## Does it tip over backwards?

Lid side (printed parts as sliced + M5Stack Tab5 120.9 g + half the hinges): **216 g**, centre of mass 50.7 mm in front of the hinge axis when closed. Base without the keyboard: **64 g**. ## Does the lid stay shut?

The two HG-MF08 are free-stop friction hinges: together 0.157 N·m (0.118..0.196 with their ±25 %). The lid side's moment about the axis is at most 0.108 N·m (level, 216 g at 50.7 mm), so the hinges hold it at any angle (1.1x at least) and it never falls shut by itself.

Let go, friction hinges turn back a little (the twist stored in them and in their seats). Anywhere else that is made up for by pushing a little further; at the closed position the lid lands on the tray and cannot go further, so it comes up again: every degree at the axis is 1.7 mm at the front edge. The snap is what keeps it down.

The lid's underside leans up toward the hinges (`PART_RELIEF`, 0.4 mm at the back face), so the rims never meet next to the hinge axis: on the PaperDIY version of this case, built first, rims touching 1.65 mm from the axis levered the lid open as soon as the hinge screws were tight.

The snap's figures are simulated: `tools/snap_sim.py` closes the built lid on the built tray along the hinge's arc, with the lip as a spring (plate model) and friction 0.3 on the ramps; all cases are in `out/snap_sim.md`.

| | at the front edge |
| --- | --- |
| push from the hinges' spring-back, less the lid's weight | 0.1..0.9 N |
| to close (the lid 0.2 mm forward .. where it was drawn) | 3..10 N |
| the snap holds (the same two cases) | 6..13 N |
| the snap holds if the lip's printed gap is 0.10 mm wider than drawn | 9 N |
| the snap holds with the lid 0.4 mm forward | 0 N (the bump does not reach its groove) |
| to open: snap + hinge friction - the lid's weight | about 6..14 N |

Every 0.1 mm the lid sits forward of its drawn position takes 0.1 mm off the snap's engagement (0.37 mm on the built parts). If the closed lid's front face stands proud of the tray's by more than about 0.2 mm, measure the step: the lip has to be moved by that much. The forces are the plate model's (ceiling and end walls rigid), so the real ones are somewhat lower. The snap coupons of plate 0 take in the whole flex zone: click them together and pull them apart before printing the lid and the tray. Too hard to open: file the bump's upper flank a little.

**Touching the screen.** A touch pushes the open lid back, the same way its weight does past 90 degrees. What the hinges' friction leaves for a finger at the middle of the screen (54 mm from the axis), with the hinges at the low end of their tolerance .. as specified:

| opening | a push the lid stands |
| --- | --- |
| 100 deg | 1.8..2.6 N |
| 120 deg | 1.2..1.9 N |
| 135 deg | 0.8..1.5 N |

A tap is about 0.5..1 N, a firm press more: with HG-MF08 hinges at the weak end of their tolerance the lid can creep back under firm presses, until it rests on the table. Stiffer hinges need other seats (not drawn).

Press the front of the lid until the snap clicks; a lid that is only laid down stays open by the spring-back. To open, hold the base: it weighs 64 g plus the keyboard, less than the pull on the lid, and the hinges would carry it up with the lid unless the keyboard weighed about 332 g.
